Zak Kohler (Nov 04 2026 at 05:03):

Basically we are right at threshold of llvm's inlining discretion. Had to fiddle/measure to get out just right.

LLM summary:

Sure! The fast path in #12823 only pays off when LLVM inlines roc_builtins_str_concat at the call site. That keeps the small strings' words in registers across a chain of concats (about 15 cycles a concat, against about 90 on main). Inlining is LLVM's cost-model call, and the final wrapper is close to the line: at 74 IR instructions it's inlined at 44 of 44 sites on my test page, while an earlier 88-instruction version was inlined at only 16 of 43. So an innocent edit to the wrapper, an LLVM upgrade, or a change in how codegen calls it could quietly drop it out of line. Strings would still be correct, and it would still beat main (variants inlined nowhere measured −13% on the page against −30%), but most of the win would be gone and nothing would fail.
So the question is whether you'd want either:
a test that builds a small app at --opt=speed and asserts the optimized IR has no call @roc_builtins_str_concat in the hot function (the IR tests in MonoLlvmCodeGen.zig check pre-optimization IR, so this would need a post-opt hook), or
codegen marking that builtin alwaysinline, so it holds by construction, with the trade-off you already cap in #11451 if the wrapper ever grows.
