Topic: Release candidate: Roc 0.1.0-RC1

Richard Feldman (Nov 21 2026 at 20:27):

We now have an official Release Candidate for Roc 0.1.0! The commit is 15177e91f6 and the binaries are on GitHub.

This is not the official 0.1.0 release yet; the "candidate" part means "this could be 0.1.0, but we're going to try it out decide whether to accept it or to make revisions and repeat this process with 0.1.0-RC2."

I am assuming we'll make revisions for RC1, but RC2 might turn out to be the one. We'll see! The main reason I want to put RC1 out there is to kick off this RC process, since we've never done one before.

we have to draw some lines for what's in 0.1.0 and what will be afterwards, so here are the lines!

between here and 0.1.0 itself, my plan is:

  • The existing feature freeze remains in effect; no new features
  • No breaking API changes to stdlib, host boundary, or anything else - unless it's unavoidable to fix a bug we decide is important
  • Don't land any more performance improvements - not compiler performance improvements, and also not performance of Roc programs themselves. I intend to do an 0.1.1 shortly after 0.1.0, and performance improvements can be back on the table then.
  • For bugfixes, the priority is fixing bugs that are both blocking people's real-world Roc programs (including hobby projects and learning materials - I mean "real-world" as opposed to theoretical or contrived) and also can't be worked around in those real-world Roc programs.
    • Bugfixes for things other than real-world blockers that can't be worked around, even for things like compiler crashes, will be deferred to 0.1.1 by default.
  • we may make exceptions on a case-by-case basis but I want the bar for exceptions to be high.

I'd love it if you could try out 0.1.0-RC1 and report any bugs you find on it in the usual way! We'll take care of the logistics of figuring out whether to include their fixes in 0.1.0-RC2 or deferring until 0.1.1, and in general my philosophy is "the more bug reports, the better" because it tells us what needs fixing! :smile:

I don't know when exactly 0.1.0 will be done, but we're getting close, and the first step is to see what bugs people are hitting in RC1!

Richard Feldman (Nov 21 2026 at 21:17):

  • cold means "empty the cache and then build a tiny hello world app, so that only the builtins are in cache"
  • warm means "immediately build again, when the cache is totally warm"
  • root changed means "change main.roc and build again, so the only cache miss is main.roc"
  • import changed means "change whatever module is imported the most in the project, and build again, to get the most number of cache misses possible from changing a single file"

Richard Feldman (Nov 21 2026 at 21:18):

so "import changed" is usually close-ish to "cold" because if a module is used in so many places across the app that basically the entire cache is invalidated, then you're practically in "cold build" territory anyway :smile:

Richard Feldman (Nov 21 2026 at 21:24):

some observations:

  • both roc check and roc build --opt=dev are sub-second for all of these, even on cold builds
  • cache makes a huge difference; "warm" is under 100ms for all of these, but of course "rebuild without changing anything" also comes up the least often in practice.
  • aside from one outlier project, "root changed" (so, only main.roc was edited) is also under 100ms for all of these
